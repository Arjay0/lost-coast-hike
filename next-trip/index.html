    How to use this

    Every list is ranked the same way: how often a trip shows up across a broad set of “best backpacking” articles, then whether it actually fits three people at 2 mph for 2–6 nights, then whether the permit is winnable. Numbers are quoted as the sources give them. Where sources disagree, the range is shown. Anything marked unverified gets checked before anyone books.

    1. Read the list that matters to you. Star anything you'd actually do.
    2. Copy your shortlist from the left and drop it in the group text.
    3. The calendar at the bottom is the constraint: the good US permits are decided between December and March.

    Where judgment overrode the raw tally: Point Reyes, Henry Coe, and Lost Coast topped the California count and were dropped on purpose (done, or too close to home for what this list is for). Wonderland tops the US count but the full loop is 9–14 days, so it's ranked as a section. Everest Base Camp and Kilimanjaro top the world count but are guided-only, so they're off; the Inca Trail is here as its self-supported cousin, Salkantay.

    List 2 · 15 trips

    Continental US, outside California

    Fly-to trips of 3–6 nights. Zion, Bryce, and the Grand Canyon get their own note further down because they came up first.

    Zion, Bryce, and the Grand Canyon

    Grand Canyon earns its place at #3 and is the most-named single hike in America. The problem is timing: on August 29, 2026 a flash flood destroyed nearly all the footbridges on Bright Angel Creek and knocked out the Transcanyon Waterline. As of early September the North Kaibab below the trailhead, Phantom Ranch, Bright Angel and Cottonwood campgrounds, both river bridges, and the lower South Kaibab and Bright Angel trails are closed with no reopening date, and corridor permits are out of the lottery until further notice. The waterline project targets 2027. Grand Canyon Lodge burned in 2025, so the North Rim has no lodging. For 2027, plan a south-side route like Hermit–Tonto–Bright Angel unless the corridor reopens.

    Zion has no multi-night trip that any list names. The Trans-Zion Trek is a 48-mile, 5-day traverse from Lee Pass to the East Entrance with a shuttle between ends, and no list mentions it. The West Rim top-down is a one-nighter (16.2 mi, 3,156 ft of descent, 9 sites, water only at Cabin Spring). The Narrows top-down is 16 mi with a one-night maximum and closes above 120 CFS. Permits release in 3-month blocks on the 5th of Dec, Mar, Jun, and Sep at 10 am MT, half reservable, $20 + $7 a person a night, and go within minutes. Verdict: great park, not a top backpack.

    Bryce is the Under-the-Rim Trail: 22.9 mi, 5,500 ft, 2–3 days, 7 sites, carry your water. $10 a permit + $5 a person and among the least competitive permits in the park system. Two mentions. A good two-night add-on to a Utah week, not a destination.

    List 3 · 12 treks

    Outside the continental US

    4–8 nights, tent and food on our backs where the trail allows it. Alaska and Hawaii count as outside. Ranked by mentions, then by whether it can be done self-supported in a week.

    Not possible right now

    • Chilkoot Trail: the international border at Chilkoot Pass remains closed to through travel, so it's out-and-back only on either side.
    • Huemul Circuit (Argentina): 64–66 km over 4 days with two Tyrolean traverses that need a harness, in 100+ km/h wind. Outside what this group signed up for.
    • Berg Lake (Mt Robson, BC): reopened in 2025 after the 2021 flood; 2 nights in, bookings open early December. A good add-on to a Rockies trip, not a destination trek.

    Japan, for the November family trip

    The Kumano Kodo Nakahechi shows up on five lists: about 70 km over 5 days from Takijiri to Hongu to Nachi, well signed and self-guided, but inn-to-inn with meals, not a tent trip. Book through Kumano Travel 3–6 months ahead for autumn. Late November works, with highs around 50°F and lows in the mid-30s. A 3-day Takijiri-to-Hongu core fits a family schedule. The Japan Alps are not a November option; the huts and Kamikochi itself close by mid-month.

    Two things that fall out of all this

    • This fall: Desolation Wilderness loses its quota on September 30. Lake Aloha is the easy October trip if we want one.
    • 2027: if Teton Crest is the target, the reservation window is the first Wednesday of January and it is gone in minutes. The plan needs the walk-up fallback (two thirds of sites are walk-up, issued the day before in Jackson) and Cirque of the Towers as plan B on the same flight.
